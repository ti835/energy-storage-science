## Abstract

Tensor network states (TNS) provide a family of efficiently compressed approximations to physically relevant many-body quantum states. An important quantity that characterizes the computational resources required by a TNS is its bond dimension. While in one dimension an area law for the Renyi-0 entropy guarantees an exact matrix product state representation with a bond dimension independent of the system size, an analogous statement does not hold for general states in two dimensions (2D).
